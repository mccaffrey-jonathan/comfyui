# Legal requirements for identifying / labeling AI-generated images — status as of 13 September 2026

Research note for a globally distributed image-generation tool. Covers the EU AI Act (Art. 50), California (SB 942 / AB 853 / pending SB 1000 and related statutes), and a survey of other regimes plus the technical standards (C2PA 2.x, IPTC DigitalSourceType) the laws lean on.

**Method / caveat.** The research sandbox blocked direct fetches of most primary sources (EUR-Lex, digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, cv.iptc.org, spec.c2pa.org, etc.). Every claim below is tied to a URL, but the text was obtained through search-engine summaries of those pages and of law-firm/press analyses rather than by reading the primary document end-to-end. Verbatim statutory quotes are marked as such where the source reproduced them; a few items are explicitly flagged "verify against primary text."

---

## 0. Three different things the laws ask for (vocabulary used throughout)

| Term | What it is | Which law demands it |
|---|---|---|
| **Latent / machine-readable marking** ("implicit label", "watermark") | Imperceptible signal or signed metadata that a machine can detect; the stronger versions must survive editing, re-encoding, screenshots | EU Art. 50(2) (providers); California §22757.3(b) "latent disclosure" (must be "permanent or extraordinarily difficult to remove"); China "implicit label"; Korea (machine-readable form acceptable for non-deepfake generative output); India (metadata / unique identifier) |
| **Manifest / visible label** ("explicit label") | Human-perceptible text, icon or audio disclaimer | EU Art. 50(4) (deployers, deepfakes and public-interest text); California §22757.3(a) "manifest disclosure" (user-selectable option; SB 1000 would delete it); China "explicit label" (mandatory); Korea (mandatory for deepfake-like output); India ("clearly and prominently labelled") |
| **Provenance data** (C2PA-style signed manifest) | Cryptographically signed metadata recording who/what/when produced or edited the asset | Not mandated by name anywhere, but it is the "digitally signed metadata" layer of the EU Code of Practice (Measure 1.1.1), the de-facto way to satisfy California's latent-disclosure field list, and what AB 853 requires large online platforms to *surface* (from 1 Jan 2027) and capture-device makers to *embed* (from 1 Jan 2028) |

## 1. European Union — AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

### 1.1 Article 50 — text and structure

Article 50 ("Transparency obligations for providers and deployers of certain AI systems") has seven paragraphs. The two that matter for an image generator:

**Art. 50(2) — provider-side machine-readable marking.** Providers of AI systems, "including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, shall ensure that the outputs of the AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated." Providers "shall ensure their technical solutions are effective, interoperable, robust and reliable as far as this is technically feasible, taking into account the specificities and limitations of various types of content, the costs of implementation and the generally acknowledged state of the art, as may be reflected in relevant technical standards." The obligation does not apply "to the extent the AI systems perform an assistive function for standard editing or do not substantially alter the input data provided by the deployer or the semantics thereof, or where authorised by law to detect, prevent, investigate or prosecute criminal offences."
Sources: [artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); [Stibbe, "Watermarking AI content under Art. 50(2)"](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/water-marking-the-machine-making-ai-generated-content-detectable); [Commission FAQ on Art. 50](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act).

**Art. 50(4) — deployer-side deepfake disclosure.** "Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates image, audio or video content constituting a deep fake shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated." Where the content "forms part of an evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous work or programme," the obligation is limited to disclosing the existence of such content "in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work." A second sub-paragraph covers AI-generated text "published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest" (not applicable to images).
Sources: [Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/eu-ai-act-guidance-expands-ai-disclosure-rules-for-advertisers-and-pr-teams/); [artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); [Privacy World, "How deep is your fake?" (July 2026)](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2026/07/how-deep-is-your-fake-a-3-minute-guide-on-labelling-obligations-under-the-eu-ai-act/).

**Art. 50(5) — timing/manner.** Information under paras 1-4 must be provided "in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure." The Guidelines read "first interaction" as covering each new natural person exposed to the output. Source: [artificialintelligenceact.eu — practical guide](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/); [Naaia](https://naaia.ai/en/ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-guidelines/).

**Art. 50(7) — codes of practice.** The Commission "shall encourage and facilitate the drawing up of codes of practice at Union level to facilitate the effective implementation of the obligations regarding the detection, marking and labelling of artificially generated or manipulated content." This is the legal hook for the Code of Practice in §1.4. Source: [Commission policy page on the Code](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content).

**Deep fake definition, Art. 3(60):** "AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video content that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful." Source: [SRD Rechtsanwälte](https://www.srd-rechtsanwaelte.de/en/blog/deepfake-labelling-under-the-ai-act-what-the-new-eu-guidance-clarifies); [Schjødt](https://schjodt.com/news/deep-fakes-in-the-ai-act).

### 1.2 Recital 133 — the named techniques

Recital 133 explains 50(2): generative systems raise "new risks of misinformation and manipulation at scale, fraud, impersonation and consumer deception," so providers should "embed technical solutions that enable marking in a machine readable format and detection that the output has been generated or manipulated by an AI system and not a human." Techniques should be "sufficiently reliable, interoperable, effective and robust as far as this is technically feasible, taking into account available techniques or a combination of such techniques, **such as watermarks, metadata identifications, cryptographic methods for proving provenance and authenticity of content, logging methods, fingerprints or other techniques, as may be appropriate**." Sources: [artificialintelligenceact.eu/recital/133](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/recital/133/); [Imatag summary](https://www.imatag.com/blog/ai-act-legal-requirement-to-label-ai-generated-content); [Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/can-copyright-law-benefit-from-the-marking-requirement-of-the-ai-act/).

C2PA is not named in the Regulation; it is the "cryptographic methods for proving provenance" example in practice, and commentators (and the Code of Practice) treat "C2PA-signed metadata + robust invisible watermark" as the current state-of-the-art combination. Sources: [Lumethic](https://www.lumethic.com/en/articles/eu-ai-act-c2pa-mandate); [C2PA Viewer — EU AI Act & Content Credentials](https://c2paviewer.com/articles/eu-ai-act-content-credentials).

### 1.3 Dates — what applied on 2 August 2026 and the Omnibus grace period

* Article 50 applies from **2 August 2026**. It was **not** postponed by the "Digital Omnibus on AI" (which pushed high-risk obligations into 2027/2028). Sources: [aiactblog.nl](https://www.aiactblog.nl/en/posts/article-50-transparency-deadline-2-august-2026); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [CSA research note, 29 Jul 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-20260729/).
* The Omnibus, **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744**, was published in the OJ on **24 July 2026** and entered into force **27 July 2026**. Sources: [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act); [EUR-Lex 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj); [NicFab](https://www.nicfab.eu/en/posts/digital-omnibus-ai-official-journal/).
* **Grace period, narrow:** generative AI systems **placed on the market before 2 August 2026** have until **2 December 2026** to comply with the Art. 50(2) marking/detection duty. It covers only 50(2); deployer duties (50(4)) and interaction disclosure (50(1)) applied on 2 Aug 2026 regardless. A system first placed on the market on/after 2 Aug 2026 must mark from day one. Sources: [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [ComplianceHub "grace period ends 2 Dec 2026"](https://compliancehub.wiki/eu-ai-act-december-2-2026-legacy-systems-watermarking-grace-period-expiry/); [Legalithm](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/eu-ai-act-what-applies-2-august-2026); [Commission quick facts](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/quick-facts-transparency-rules-ai-systems).
* Enforcement is by **national market surveillance authorities**; the AI Office coordinates. Source: [Bird & Bird on final Guidelines](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/european-commission-adopts-final-guidelines-on-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-first-impr).

### 1.4 Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content (the "marking and labelling" code)

Timeline: work launched Nov 2025; **first draft 17 Dec 2025**; **second draft 5 Mar 2026**; **final published 10 Jun 2026**; drawn up by six independent experts under AI Office facilitation; the **Commission and AI Board confirmed it "adequate"** for demonstrating Art. 50 compliance in July 2026; **~190 signatories** by end-July 2026 (sign-up encouraged by 22 Jul 2026 for the initial list). Section-1 (provider) signatories include Aleph Alpha, Anthropic, **Black Forest Labs**, Cohere, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Mistral, OpenAI and Synthesia; Midjourney and Stability AI were not confirmed in sources. Sources: [Commission news, final Code](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content); [first draft](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-first-draft-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content); [second draft](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-second-draft-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content); [Faegre Drinker on adequacy](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations); [Commission "strong backing"](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/strong-backing-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content); [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/eu-commission-publishes-ai-transparency-code-of-practice.html); [CADE, 190 signatories](https://cadeproject.org/updates/eu-transparency-code-for-ai-generated-content-signed-by-190-signatories/); [ai-resources.eu](https://ai-resources.eu/soft-law/transparency-code-of-practice/).

Structure and substance:

* **Section 1 — providers (Art. 50(2)).** **Commitment 1, Measure 1.1: multi-layered marking — at least two machine-readable layers.** Sub-measure 1.1.1 **digitally-signed metadata** (record whether content is AI-generated/manipulated; digitally signed and time-stamped, tamper-evident — i.e., a C2PA-style manifest). Sub-measure 1.1.2 **imperceptible watermark** embedded in the content. Sub-measure 1.1.3 (optional supplement) **fingerprinting / logging** against a registry so content can be re-identified after alteration. Fingerprinting and forensic detection "may supplement but cannot replace" the two baseline layers. Providers must also make **detection solutions** available; providers with **fewer than 1,000,000 monthly users** may charge a limited fee in high-cost cases. For text, the watermark applies to outputs longer than ~200 tokens (images have no such carve-out). Sources: [IPTC on the Code](https://iptc.org/news/eu-ai-transparency-code-of-practice-june-2026/); [ComplianceHub — watermarks and metadata](https://compliancehub.wiki/eu-ai-act-marking-labelling-code-of-practice-article-50-2026/); [TechPolicy.Press explainer](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-eus-ai-transparency-code-of-practice-explained/); [Hayes Solicitors](https://hayes-solicitors.ie/news/marking-and-labelling-of-ai-generated-content/); [Bird & Bird — final Code](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/taking-the-eu-ai-act-to-practice-the-final-transparency-code-of-practice); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/07/what-the-eus-new-code-of-practice-means).
* **Section 2 — deployers (Art. 50(4)).** Deepfakes (and public-interest text) are to be labelled with the **EU "AI" icon set published by the AI Office** (Annex 1) or an equivalent, ideally supplemented by whether the content was *generated* or *manipulated*; labels must be clear, accessible, visible at first exposure, and persist through the distribution chain; audio disclaimers where a visual label is impossible; plus internal processes, training, correction of missing labels, and cooperation with authorities. Sources: [Commission — EU icons for labelling AI-generated content](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-icons-labelling-ai-generated-content); [TechPolicy.Press on deepfake labelling](https://www.techpolicy.press/what-the-eus-new-ai-code-of-practice-means-for-labeling-deepfakes/); [Lexology summary of the final Code](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=f7b2a1c6-c053-45e5-b5d6-83411c67b0d9); [Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2026/technology/eu-ai-act-code-practice-marking-labelling-ai-generated-content/).
* Legal effect: voluntary, but signing plus implementation gives a presumption of conformity / "adequate means" of demonstrating compliance. Source: [Bird & Bird — final Code](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/taking-the-eu-ai-act-to-practice-the-final-transparency-code-of-practice); [Reed Smith on adequacy decision](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/).

### 1.5 Commission Guidelines on Article 50

* Draft published for consultation (May 2026); **final adopted 20 July 2026**, reference **C(2026) 5054 final**, ~51 pages; addressed to national authorities, providers and deployers; no new obligations, but the reference document market-surveillance authorities will use. Sources: [Commission library page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems); [Commission policy page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-transparency-obligations); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/european-commission-adopts-final-guidelines-on-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-first-impr); [regulation-ai.eu](https://www.regulation-ai.eu/en/transparency-obligations/guidelines/); [Legalithm — what changes before 2 Aug](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/article-50-final-guidelines-what-changes-before-2-august).
* Key readings relevant to an image tool:
  * **Open-source is not exempt** from Art. 50 (the GPAI open-source relief in Art. 53 does not extend to Art. 50 system obligations). Source: [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations); [Shibolet](https://www.shibolet.com/en/eu-ai-act-article-50/).
  * **Whoever places the *system* on the market is the provider under 50(2)** — a company that wraps a third-party or open-weight model (API or local weights) into a user-facing generator is a provider of a GPAI *system* and must ensure marking. Source: [regulation-ai.eu — transparency obligations](https://www.regulation-ai.eu/en/transparency-obligations/); [McCann FitzGerald, Part 1 provider obligations](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/data-privacy-and-cyber-risk/ai-transparency-european-commissions-guidelines-on-article-50-part-1-provider-obligations).
  * **"Assistive function for standard editing"** exemption covers operations that do not alter the informational substance (spell-check, standard retouching); generative inpainting/outpainting that changes meaning does not qualify. Source: [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations).
  * **Law-enforcement exemption** to 50(2) requires the use to be permitted under the rules governing the authority's powers, not an ad-hoc statute. Source: [ava's blog on LE exemption](https://blog.avas.space/le-exempt-draft-aiact/).
  * **Artistic/creative carve-out (50(4))** is read narrowly; purely informative or commercial deepfakes get no relief; the disclosure may sit in accompanying material rather than on the image. Source: [Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/eu-ai-act-guidance-expands-ai-disclosure-rules-for-advertisers-and-pr-teams/).
  * Harmonised standards from **CEN-CENELEC JTC 21** are still in progress; until then the Code is the practical conformity route. Source: [Tech Jacks — what to build](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-brief/eu-ai-act-article-50-compliance-what-to-build/).

### 1.6 Penalties — Article 99

* Art. 50 breaches fall under **Art. 99(4)**: administrative fines up to **EUR 15,000,000 or 3 % of total worldwide annual turnover** of the preceding financial year, whichever is **higher**. Prohibited-practice breaches (Art. 5) are 35 M / 7 %; supplying incorrect information 7.5 M / 1 %. Sources: [activeMind.legal Art. 99](https://www.activemind.legal/legislation/ai-act/article-99/); [AI Act Service Desk Art. 99](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-99); [aiactbase.eu](https://aiactbase.eu/ai-act-penalties-fines/).
* **SMEs and start-ups:** each fine is capped at the percentage or the fixed amount, **whichever is lower** (Art. 99(6)). Source: [activeMind.legal](https://www.activemind.legal/legislation/ai-act/article-99/); [euaiactchecklist](https://euaiactchecklist.com/eu-ai-act-fines-penalties.html).
* Fines are imposed by Member-State authorities under national procedures; the AI Office fines only GPAI *model* providers (Art. 101).

### 1.7 Exemptions checklist (EU)

1. Assistive/standard editing that does not substantially alter input or its meaning (50(2)).
2. Systems authorised by law for criminal-law enforcement (50(2), 50(4)).
3. Artistic/creative/satirical/fictional works: reduced, not eliminated, deployer disclosure (50(4)).
4. Grace to 2 Dec 2026 for legacy systems (marking duty only).
5. No SME, open-source or non-commercial exemption from Art. 50 itself (only the fine cap).

## 2. California

### 2.1 California AI Transparency Act — SB 942 (2024) as amended by AB 853 (2025); Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 22757–22757.6

**Enactment and dates.** SB 942 (Becker) signed **19 Sep 2024**, originally operative 1 Jan 2026. **AB 853 (Wicks)** signed **13 Oct 2025**, moved the operative date to **2 August 2026** (explicitly to align with the EU) and added platform/device obligations phased to 2027–2028. Sources: [Governor's 19 Sep 2024 release](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2024/09/19/governor-newsom-signs-bills-to-crack-down-on-sexually-explicit-deepfakes-require-ai-watermarking/); [Troutman](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2025/10/california-ai-transparency-act-amendments-signed-into-law/); [Hintze Law](https://hintzelaw.com/blog/2025/10/19/california-amends-artificial-intelligence-transparency-act-and-passes-ai-defenses-act); [AB 853 text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB853); [SB 942 text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB942); [Morgan Lewis, Aug 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/08/new-california-ai-disclosure-rules-become-operative); [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/california-sb-942-ai-transparency-act-effective-august-2026).

**Covered provider (§22757.1).** A person that creates, codes, or otherwise produces a GenAI system that has **over 1,000,000 monthly visitors or users** and is **publicly accessible within California**. Source: [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-tech/zooming-in-on-ai-california-evolving-ai-legal-landscape-entering-2026); [Jones Day 2024](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2024/10/california-enacts-ai-transparency-law-requiring-disclosures-for-ai-content).

**Duties operative 2 Aug 2026 (§22757.2–.3):**

1. **Free AI detection tool (§22757.2).** Must let a user assess whether image/video/audio was created or altered by the provider's system; accept **uploads and URLs**; **output any system provenance data detected** but **not output personal provenance data**; be **publicly accessible** with an **API** for programmatic use; **collect user feedback**; provider **may not retain** personal information or content beyond what is needed and may not retain personal provenance data. Sources: [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/california-establishes-ai-transparency-act); [Cowan DeBaets](https://cdas.com/all-about-californias-new-ai-transparency-act/); [TrustArc](https://trustarc.com/resource/california-ai-transparency-laws-sb942-ab2013/).
   * *System provenance data* = provenance data not reasonably capable of being associated with a particular user, containing information about the type of device, system or service used to generate content (and/or whether it was AI-generated/altered). *Personal provenance data* = provenance data containing personal information or unique device/user identifiers. Source: [Cowan DeBaets](https://cdas.com/all-about-californias-new-ai-transparency-act/).
2. **Manifest disclosure option (§22757.3(a)).** Provider must offer users the option to include a visible disclosure that identifies the content as AI-generated; it must be clear, conspicuous, appropriate for the medium, understandable to a reasonable person, and permanent or extraordinarily difficult to remove to the extent technically feasible. Source: [SB 942 text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB942); [Jenner & Block](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/publications/californias-ai-transparency-act).
3. **Latent disclosure — mandatory (§22757.3(b)).** In all AI-generated image, video or audio the system creates (or alters), a latent disclosure that, **to the extent technically feasible and reasonable**, conveys:
   * the **name of the covered provider**;
   * the **name and version number of the GenAI system** that created or altered the content;
   * the **time and date** of creation or alteration;
   * a **unique identifier**.
   The disclosure must be **detectable by the provider's AI detection tool**, **consistent with widely accepted industry standards**, and **permanent or extraordinarily difficult to remove** to the extent technically feasible. Sources: [FindLaw §22757.3](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-22757-3/); [Justia §22757.3](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-8/chapter-25/section-22757-3/); [Orrick compiled text](https://infobytes.orrick.com/wp-content/uploads/California-AI-Transparency-Act-and-Amendment2.pdf); [AI Compliance Atlas](https://aicomplianceatlas.com/law/california-sb-942).
   * "Widely accepted industry standards" is universally read as C2PA Content Credentials; the "extraordinarily difficult to remove" requirement points to pairing the manifest with a robust watermark (a bare XMP/C2PA block is trivially stripped). Sources: [Secure Privacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/california-ai-transparency-law); [Neomanex — Midjourney lacks C2PA](https://neomanex.com/news/ca-ai-transparency-act-operative); [OneTrust](https://www.onetrust.com/blog/california-ai-transparency-act-requirements-deadlines-and-compliance-steps/).
4. **Licensee flow-down (§22757.3(c)).** If the provider licenses the system to a third party it must contractually require the licensee to maintain the disclosure capability; if the provider learns the licensee has disabled it, the provider must **revoke the licence within 96 hours**, and the licensee must cease use on revocation. Source: [Orrick, contract requirements](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/01/Navigating-the-California-AI-Transparency-Act-New-Contract-Requirements); [Vorp Labs checklist](https://vorplabs.com/ai-regulatory-updates/california-ai-transparency-act-checklist).

**Exemption (§22757.5).** The chapter does not apply to any product, service, website or application that provides **exclusively non-user-generated video game, television, streaming, movie, or interactive experiences**. Source: [Jones Day 2024](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2024/10/california-enacts-ai-transparency-law-requiring-disclosures-for-ai-content); [regulations.ai](https://regulations.ai/regulations/california-sb-942-ai-transparency-act-2024).

**Penalties and enforcement (§22757.4).** Civil penalty of **$5,000 per violation**; **each day** of violation is a separate violation; enforceable by the **Attorney General, a city attorney, or a county counsel** (no private right of action). Sources: [Kolmogorov Law](https://www.kolmogorovlaw.com/california-ai-transparency-act-sb-942); [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/california-ai-transparency-act-enforcement-august-2026/); [AI Laws by State](https://www.ailawsbystate.com/blog/california-ai-transparency-act-sb-942).

**AB 853 additions (phased):**

* **GenAI hosting platforms — from 1 Jan 2027:** may not knowingly make available a covered provider's GenAI system that does not place the required latent disclosures. Source: [regulations.ai — AB 853](https://regulations.ai/regulations/RAI-US-CA-AB85300-2025); [Troutman](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2025/10/california-ai-transparency-act-amendments-signed-into-law/).
* **Large online platforms — from 1 Jan 2027.** Definition: public-facing social media platform, file-sharing platform, mass messaging platform or stand-alone search engine that distributes content to users who did not create it and exceeded **2,000,000 unique monthly users** in the preceding 12 months (excludes broadband and telecom services). Duties: **detect** whether provenance data is embedded in content distributed on the platform; give users a **UI disclosing** whether content is AI-generated or from a capture device (where system provenance data is available); let users **inspect system provenance data** via the interface, by download, or via a third-party link; **not knowingly strip** provenance data or digital signatures; do not surface personal provenance data. Sources: [Troutman](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2025/10/california-ai-transparency-act-amendments-signed-into-law/); [regulations.ai](https://regulations.ai/regulations/RAI-US-CA-AB85300-2025); [ETO AGORA](https://agora.eto.tech/instrument/5006); [EDRM, Aug 2026](https://edrm.net/2026/08/californias-ai-transparency-act-arrives-alongside-europes-article-50/).
* **Capture device manufacturers — from 1 Jan 2028.** Capture device = device that can record photographs, audio or video (cameras, phones, voice recorders) sold in California. Manufacturers must offer users the option to include, and **embed by default**, a latent disclosure in captured content conveying the **manufacturer name, device name/version, and time/date** of capture. Source: [Troutman](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2025/10/california-ai-transparency-act-amendments-signed-into-law/); [Senate Judiciary analysis of AB 853](https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2025-07/ab-853-wicks-sjud-analysis.pdf).

### 3.1 China — Measures for Labeling AI-Generated Synthetic Content + GB 45438-2025

* Issued 14 Mar 2025 by CAC, MIIT, MPS and NRTA; **effective 1 Sep 2025**; built on the Cybersecurity Law, Algorithmic Recommendation Provisions, Deep Synthesis Provisions (Arts. 16-17) and the GenAI Measures. Sources: [China Law Translate (full text)](https://www.chinalawtranslate.com/en/ai-labeling/); [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/chinas-ai-labeling-measures-and-mandatory-national-standards-take-effect-september-1); [Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/international/china/china-releases-new-labeling-requirements-for-ai-generated-content/); [Bird & Bird comparison with EU](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/new-ai-content-labelling-rules-in-china-what-are-they-and-how-do-they-compare-to-the-eu-ai-act).
* **Art. 4 — explicit (visible) labels, mandatory** for text, images, audio, video and virtual scenes: images need a clear label in an appropriate area; video needs a prominent label at the start and during playback; audio a voice prompt or rhythm cue; and files offered for **download, copy or export must carry the explicit label**. Source: [ETO AGORA](https://agora.eto.tech/instrument/2096); [Harris Sliwoski](https://harris-sliwoski.com/chinalawblog/chinas-new-ai-labeling-rules-what-every-china-business-needs-to-know/).
* **Art. 5 — implicit labels in file metadata, mandatory**: must include the **content's attributes (AI-generated), the service provider's name or code, and a content ID number**; digital watermarks are *encouraged* but not mandated. Source: [ETO AGORA](https://agora.eto.tech/instrument/2096); [Regulations.ai](https://regulations.ai/regulations/RAI-CN-NA-MIASCXX-2025).
* **GB 45438-2025** (mandatory national standard, "Cybersecurity technology — Labeling method for content generated by AI"), effective 1 Sep 2025, specifies the metadata as **XMP under namespace `http://www.tc260.org.cn/ns/AIGC/1.0/`** with fields **`Label`, `ContentProducer`, `ProduceID`, `ReservedCode1`, `ContentPropagator`, `PropagateID`, `ReservedCode2`** (ReservedCode1 is for producer-side integrity/security data; the propagator fields are filled by the distributing platform), covering HEIF/HEIC, JPEG, PNG, TIFF, WebP and GIF for images, plus watermark formats per media type. Sources: [Geopolitechs](https://www.geopolitechs.org/p/chinas-mandatory-national-standards); [Digital Policy Alert on TC260 image guidelines](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/33153-tc260-adopted-guidelines-on-identification-methods-for-ai-generated-synthetic-image-content); [DGC Briefings](https://dgcbriefings.substack.com/p/china-finalised-labelling-methods); [HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/tmt/2025-posts/China-Releases-Laws-to-Mandate-Labelling-of-AIGC); [standard listing](https://www.chinesestandard.net/PDF/English.aspx/GB45438-2025). (Exact explicit-label sizing rules live in the standard text — verify against the primary document.)
* Platforms must verify labels and tag content as confirmed / possibly / suspected AI-generated; users must self-declare; nobody may delete, alter or forge labels; app stores must check labeling. Penalties come from the underlying laws rather than the Measures. Enforcement is active: WeChat/Douyin rolled out self-declaration and auto-labeling on day one; Douyin reported penalising ~14,000 accounts in Q1 2026 for AI-labeling violations. Sources: [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/policy/article/3323959/chinas-social-media-platforms-rush-abide-ai-generated-content-labelling-law); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/09/01/chinas-top-social-media-platforms-take-steps-comply-new-ai-content-labeling-rules/); [Pertama Partners](https://www.pertamapartners.com/insights/china-ai-regulations).

### 3.2 South Korea — Framework (Basic) Act on AI

* Promulgated 21 Jan 2025; **in force 22 Jan 2026** together with its Enforcement Decree. Sources: [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2026-02-20/south-korea-comprehensive-ai-legal-framework-takes-effect); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/south-koreas-new-ai-framework-act-a-balancing-act-between-innovation-and-regulation/).
* **Art. 31 (transparency):** operators of generative or high-impact AI must notify users in advance that AI is used; **generative AI outputs must be indicated as AI-generated**; where output is "difficult to distinguish from reality" (deepfake-type image/audio/video) the operator must **clearly notify or label** it. The Decree (Art. 22) allows the general indication to be in **human- or machine-readable form** (invisible watermark / provenance metadata acceptable), but **deepfake-type output needs a label users can clearly recognise**. Exceptions where the artificial nature is obvious (e.g., cartoons/stylised art may use only an invisible watermark). Sources: [Lexology — draft enforcement decree](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=8bdc4245-efa9-4fbd-8b3d-b61a1d3ac7c6); [CODIT on the Decree](https://thecodit.com/blog/AI-act-enforcement-decree-en); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/01/29/south-korea-launches-landmark-laws-requiring-labels-on-ai-generated-content/); [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/south-korea-tightens-ai-rules-with-watermark-requirement-82d43c34).
* **Fines:** administrative fine up to **KRW 30 million** (~US$20k) (Art. 43) for failing to notify/label or to designate a domestic representative; government committed to **defer fines for at least one year** from 22 Jan 2026 except in cases of serious harm. Sources: [Stimson](https://www.stimson.org/2026/south-koreas-ai-basic-act-seeking-balance-between-industry-innovation-and-social-risk/); [DLA Piper tracker](https://intelligence.dlapiper.com/artificial-intelligence/?t=08-enforcement&c=KR); [Korea JoongAng Daily on enforcement gaps](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/koreas-groundbreaking-ai-law-requires-watermarks-on-generated-content-but-enforcement-gaps-remain/12094830).
* **Extraterritorial**; foreign operators must appoint a domestic representative above thresholds (annual revenue > KRW 1 trillion, or AI-service revenue > KRW 10 billion, or > 1 million average daily Korean users over the prior three months). Source: [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/south-korea-ai-basic-act); [Safe AI for Business](https://safeaiforbusiness.com/south-korea-ai-basic-act/).

### 3.3 India — IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Amendment Rules, 2026

Notified **10 Feb 2026**, in force **20 Feb 2026**. Introduces "synthetically generated information" (SGI). Intermediaries that enable creation of SGI must ensure it is **clearly and prominently labelled** and **embedded with permanent metadata or a unique identifier / provenance mark** tracing origin, and must not allow labels to be removed. The October 2025 draft's "10 % of surface area / first 10 % of audio" rule was **dropped** in the final text. Separately, a 3-hour takedown window applies to unlawful content. Sources: [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/india-targets-deepfakes-and-ai-generated-content-key-changes-under-meitys-2026-102mjwn); [AZB Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/88502/); [HLC](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content); [MeitY FAQ on the draft](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf); [Chandhiok & Mahajan](https://chandhiok.com/insights/cm-e-alert-ministry-of-electronics-and-information-technology-notifies-the-information-technology-intermediary-guidelines-and-digital-media-ethics-code-amendment-rules-2026).

### 3.4 Spain — Ley Orgánica para el buen uso y la gobernanza de la IA (national AI Act implementing law)

* Anteproyecto approved 11 Mar 2025; **Proyecto de Ley approved by the Council of Ministers 26 May 2026**, entered Congress 28 May, published in BOCG 12 Jun 2026 (Proyecto 121/000096); amendment period closed 30 Jun 2026; **still in parliamentary process, not in force** as of September 2026. * **Durable Content Credentials**: manifest (hard binding) + **soft binding** (invisible watermark and/or fingerprint) so the manifest can be recovered from a cloud/registry after metadata is stripped — this is exactly the two-layer structure the EU Code's Measure 1.1 requires. Sources: [C2PA Soft Binding API](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/softbinding/Decoupled.html); [CAI — Durable Content Credentials](https://opensource.contentauthenticity.org/docs/durable-cr/); [Digimarc on C2PA 2.1](https://www.digimarc.com/blog/c2pa-21-strengthening-content-credentials-digital-watermarks).
* **Conformance Program** launched mid-2025 with **Assurance Level 1** (software keys — AI platforms, editors, cloud) and **Level 2** (hardware-backed keys — cameras/phones); Google Pixel 10 first Level-2 device. Sources: [c2pa.org/conformance](https://c2pa.org/conformance/); [CAI blog](https://contentauthenticity.org/blog/raising-the-bar-for-trust-introducing-the-c2pa-conformance-program); [SSL.com submission guide](https://www.ssl.com/article/how-to-submit-to-the-c2pa-conformance-program/).
* **How generative output is declared in a manifest:** a `c2pa.actions.v2` assertion with action **`c2pa.created`** and `digitalSourceType` = `http://cv.iptc.org/newscodes/digitalsourcetype/trainedAlgorithmicMedia`, plus a `softwareAgent` {name, version}; inpainting/outpainting is recorded as `c2pa.edited` / `c2pa.placed` with `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia`. Sources: [CAI — writing assertions and actions](https://opensource.contentauthenticity.org/docs/manifest/writing/assertions-actions/); [C2PA AI/ML guidance 2.4](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/ai-ml/ai_ml.html); [C2PA digital source type page](https://c2pa.org/digitalsourcetype/trainedalgorithmicdata/).

### 3.8 IPTC DigitalSourceType vocabulary (`http://cv.iptc.org/newscodes/digitalsourcetype/<code>`)

The vocabulary is the controlled list used both in IPTC Photo Metadata (XMP `Iptc4xmpExt:DigitalSourceType`) and in C2PA `digitalSourceType`. Terms (verify definitions against [cv.iptc.org](https://cv.iptc.org/newscodes/digitalsourcetype), which could not be fetched here):

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `digitalCapture` | Original digital capture of a real-life scene (camera) |
| `computationalCapture` | Capture with computational photography (multi-frame fusion etc.) |
| `negativeFilm` / `positiveFilm` / `print` | Digitised from film negative / transparency / print |
| `humanEdits` | Original media with minor human edits |
| `minorHumanEdits` | Minor human edits (added later; check current status) |
| `compositeCapture` | Composite of captured elements |
| `algorithmicallyEnhanced` | Captured media enhanced algorithmically (e.g., AI upscaling/denoise) |
| `dataDrivenMedia` | Media generated from data (charts, data visualisations) |
| `digitalCreation` | Media created digitally by a human (illustration, CGI) — replaced retired `softwareImage` |
| `virtualRecording` | Recording of a virtual event (game capture, video call) |
| `compositeSynthetic` | Composite including synthetic elements |
| **`trainedAlgorithmicMedia`** | **Media created purely by an algorithm trained on sampled content (generative AI)** |
| **`compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia`** | **Composite including one or more generative-AI elements (inpainting/outpainting)** — added 2024 |
| `algorithmicMedia` | Media created purely by an algorithm *not* based on sampled training data (procedural) |
| `screenCapture` | Screen capture |

Sources: [IPTC synthetic-media guidance](https://iptc.org/news/iptc-publishes-metadata-guidance-for-ai-generated-synthetic-media/); [IPTC — new term for inpainting/outpainting](https://iptc.org/news/new-digital-source-type-term-added-to-support-inpainting-outpainting-in-generative-ai/); [IPTC 2024 Q3 NewsCodes release](https://iptc.org/news/newscodes-2024-q3-release-including-media-topics-and-digital-source-type-updates/); [IPTC ExifTool guidance](https://iptc.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/PMD/pages/649330691/Reading+and+writing+IPTC+metadata+on+the+command+line+using+ExifTool); [schema.org IPTCDigitalSourceEnumeration](https://schema.org/IPTCDigitalSourceEnumeration); [Google Merchant Center use of trainedAlgorithmicMedia](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/14743464?hl=en-IE).

## 4. What this means for an image-generation tool shipped worldwide (synthesis)

| Requirement | EU (Art. 50 + Code) | California (SB 942/AB 853 today) | California if SB 1000 signed | China | Korea | India |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who is on the hook for the *file* | Provider of the system (incl. anyone wrapping open weights into a product) | "Covered provider" >1 M monthly users, publicly accessible in CA | Any publicly accessible GenAI system provider | Service provider + distributing platform | AI business operator | Intermediary enabling SGI |
| Robust latent mark that survives editing | Yes — ≥2 layers: signed metadata **and** imperceptible watermark (fingerprint optional) | Yes — "permanent or extraordinarily difficult to remove," detectable by provider's own tool | Same, plus "created vs altered" flag | Metadata mandatory; watermark encouraged | Machine-readable acceptable for non-deepfake output | "Permanent metadata or unique identifier" |
| Required metadata fields | Whether generated/manipulated; signed + time-stamped (Code) | Provider name; system name + version; time & date; unique identifier | + created/altered flag | `Label`, `ContentProducer`, `ProduceID`, `ReservedCode1` (+ propagator fields) | Not prescribed | Not prescribed |
| Visible label on the image | Deployer duty for **deepfakes** only (EU icon); artistic carve-out | Must *offer* a user-selectable manifest disclosure | Manifest option **removed** | **Mandatory** explicit label on all AI images, incl. exports | **Mandatory** for realistic/deepfake output; optional for obviously artificial | **Mandatory** "clear and prominent" |
| Public detection/verification tool | Detection solution must be made available (fee allowed <1 M users) | Free public tool with API, upload+URL, feedback | "Disclosure verification tool" | Platform-side verification | — | Platform-side detection |
| Grace / start | 2 Aug 2026 (legacy systems: marking by 2 Dec 2026) | 2 Aug 2026; platforms 1 Jan 2027; devices 1 Jan 2028 | On signature (urgency) — verify | 1 Sep 2025 | 22 Jan 2026; fines deferred ≥1 yr | 20 Feb 2026 |
| Max penalty | €15 M / 3 % (SMEs: lower of) | $5,000 per violation per day | Same | Under Cybersecurity Law etc.; account bans | KRW 30 M | Loss of safe-harbour, IT Act penalties |

Practical minimum that satisfies all of the above for a generated image: (1) a C2PA 2.x manifest with `c2pa.created` + `trainedAlgorithmicMedia` (or `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia` for edits), `softwareAgent` name+version, signed timestamp, and a UUID; (2) an imperceptible watermark carrying at least a provider ID and that UUID (this is what makes the California "extraordinarily difficult to remove" and the EU "robust" tests plausible, since XMP is stripped by most social platforms); (3) IPTC `DigitalSourceType` in XMP; (4) for China, the TC260 `AIGC` XMP block; (5) an optional burned-in visible label that the user can toggle on (mandatory-on when serving China/India, and when the output depicts a real, identifiable person for Korea and the EU deployer duty).
