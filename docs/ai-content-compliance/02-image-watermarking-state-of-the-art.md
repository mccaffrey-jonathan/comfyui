# Invisible image watermarking: state of the art as of September 2026, with a focus on SynthID-Image and attacks on it

Research date: 2026-09-13. Sourcing note: in this environment arXiv, Hugging Face, OpenReview, CVF open-access, NeurIPS proceedings, Medium and most publisher domains were blocked by the egress proxy, so paper details below come from search-engine snippets of those pages, from GitHub READMEs (reachable), and from the PyPI JSON API (reachable). Where a number comes only from a snippet or a secondary source it is marked as such.

## 0. Executive summary

- **SynthID-Image is a post-hoc, pixel-space, jointly-trained encoder/decoder watermark** (not an in-generation/latent watermark). The public technical report is arXiv:2510.09263 (Gowal et al., Google DeepMind, 13 Oct 2025). Despite the framing in the request, I found **no evidence that SynthID-Image was published in Nature**; the DeepMind Nature paper is **SynthID-Text** (Dathathri et al., *Nature* 634, 818-823, Oct 2024). The external variant "SynthID-O" carries a **136-bit payload in a 512x512 image**, is evaluated against 30 transformations in 6 categories, and reports **99.98% TPR @ 0.1% FPR** (random transforms) / 99.72% (worst-case). Detection uses **non-parametric conformal p-values**; encoders are strictly versioned while decoders are continuously retrained. **Image weights are not released**; only SynthID-Text is open (Apache-2.0 code, in `transformers` >= 4.46). ([arXiv 2510.09263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09263), [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2510.09263), [synthid-text repo](https://github.com/google-deepmind/synthid-text), [Nature SynthID-Text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08025-4))
- **SynthID has been broken repeatedly in the black-box/no-box setting**: UnMarker (IEEE S&P 2025) cut SynthID detection from ~100% to ~21% (Google paid a VRP bounty); MarkNull (Aug 2026) "successfully compromises Google's SynthID-Image"; RAVEN (CVPR 2026) reports mean TPR@1%FPR of 0.026 across 15 schemes; the NeurIPS 2024 "Erasing the Invisible" competition saw top teams remove >=89% of watermarks with high quality. Forgery is also practical for many schemes (WMCopier, one-shot preference-model forging, black-box semantic forgery). The theoretical ceiling is now also known: no binary robust watermark survives >50% of its symbols being changed (Francati et al.). A May-2026 counterpoint (Goonatilake & Ateniese) shows removal leaves forensic traces detectable at >98% TPR @ 1% FPR, and a May-2026 Inria study finds **classic Broken-Arrows-style watermarking beats modern neural schemes on security** in realistic settings.
- **For a permissive, no-training, RST-robust design**, the best-known recipe is still the Digimarc/Pereira-Pun/Solachidis-Pitas family: keyed spread-spectrum in the **DFT magnitude of luminance** (translation-invariant), mid-frequency **ring/annulus** placement (rotation tolerant), an explicit **synchronization template of Fourier peaks** resolved by **log-polar resampling + phase correlation** (rotation/scale), **tiling** for crop, **JND/NVF masking** for invisibility, **normalized-correlation detection with a whitening filter**, and **FPR calibrated for the number of geometric hypotheses searched**. Details, pitfalls and library choices are in Sections 4-5.

### 1.2 What is publicly known about the architecture

From the paper's abstract and the passages that surfaced in search snippets of the arXiv HTML, plus secondary write-ups:

- **Post-hoc, model-independent, pixel-space.** A separate encoder network stamps the watermark onto an already-generated image; a decoder network detects it. The generator itself is not modified (contrast Stable Signature / Tree-Ring). This is why the same system can be applied to Imagen, Gemini image output, Veo frames, and now OpenAI's images. ([wiltodelta synthid.md summary of the paper](https://github.com/wiltodelta/remove-ai-watermarks/blob/main/docs/synthid.md), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09263v1))
- **Jointly trained encoder/decoder with distortion layers**, "optimized to spread the signal across images in ways that survive specific perturbations". The paper states adversarial training (Madry et al.) "remains largely unsolved" and that for SynthID-Image it is sufficient to enforce adversarial robustness only against adversaries with limited information (black-box) and constrained compute. ([snippet, arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09263v1))
- **Payload / keys.** The externally benchmarked variant **SynthID-O encodes 136 bits at 512x512**. Bit accuracy 99.59% under aggregated random transforms. The paper criticises TrustMark and VideoSeal for "strong variations across secrets" and "large structured artifacts" in difference maps, i.e., SynthID's embedding is meant to be secret-consistent and content-adaptive. Whether the 136 bits are a message, a key-derived codeword, or both is not spelled out in the material I could reach; the paper describes "strict encoder versioning" (watermark type fixed per encoder version) with "decoders that can be updated continuously". ([snippets](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09263v1))
- **Detection statistics.** "Non-parametric conformal p-values" are used for detection so that no distributional assumption on decoder outputs is required across content types; reported operating point is 0.1% FPR. ([snippets](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.09263))
- **Evaluation protocol.** 30 transformations in 6 categories (Color, Combination, Noise, Overlay, Quality, Spatial). SynthID-O: **99.98% TPR@0.1%FPR** aggregated-random (next best 90.62%), **99.72%** aggregated worst-case (+16.35 pp over next best). Competitors reportedly collapse to 15-76% on spatial transforms. ([snippets](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09263v1), [wiltodelta](https://github.com/wiltodelta/remove-ai-watermarks/blob/main/docs/synthid.md))
- **Threat model (Sec. 6 of the paper).** Removal, forgery, and model extraction are discussed; the paper's stated position is that robustness to unlimited white-box adversaries is not a goal. ([snippets](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.09263))
- **Scale.** >10 billion images and video frames watermarked at time of the paper (Oct 2025); Google cites >100 billion pieces of content across modalities by May 2026. ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09263), [Quartz, May 2026](https://qz.com/google-synthid-ai-detection-search-chrome-051926))

### 1.5 Attacks, reverse engineering and security results relevant to SynthID

Chronological, with what each shows against SynthID specifically vs. against the class of watermark SynthID belongs to (post-hoc learned encoder/decoder):

1. **Regeneration / diffusion purification** - Zhao et al., "Invisible Image Watermarks Are Provably Removable Using Generative AI" (NeurIPS 2024). Noise-then-denoise with a diffusion model or VAE provably removes pixel-level invisible marks (DwtDct, DwtDctSvd, RivaGAN, SSL, StegaStamp tested) with a bound tied to the perturbation budget; semantic (Tree-Ring-style) marks resist. This remains the baseline every later attack is measured against. ([arXiv 2306.01953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01953), [code](https://github.com/XuandongZhao/WatermarkAttacker))
2. **WAVES benchmark** - An, Ding et al. (ICML 2024). 26 attacks (distortions, regeneration, adversarial-embedding via CLIP/VAE/ResNet surrogates), TPR@0.1%FPR, 8 quality metrics, on Tree-Ring, Stable Signature, StegaStamp. Findings: StegaStamp most robust overall; Tree-Ring vulnerable to adversarial embedding/surrogate-detector and "rinsing" regeneration; Stable Signature falls to nearly all regeneration attacks. ([arXiv 2401.08573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08573), [site](https://wavesbench.github.io/), [code](https://github.com/umd-huang-lab/WAVES))
3. **Averaging / steganalysis** - Yang, Ci, Song, Shou, "Can Simple Averaging Defeat Modern Watermarks?" / "Steganalysis on Digital Watermarking: Is Your Defense Truly Impervious?" (NeurIPS 2024). Averaging many watermarked images (or their DDIM-inverted latents) reveals **content-agnostic** patterns; the extracted pattern removes and, for Tree-Ring, **forges** the mark. Defences: content-adaptive embedding, multi-key assignment. This is the "averaging / content-agnostic pattern extraction" attack you asked about; SynthID is content-adaptive by design and the paper does not report breaking it, but the **Reverse-SynthID** project (below) applies precisely this idea to Gemini images using solid-colour reference images. ([arXiv 2406.09026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09026), [NeurIPS PDF](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/67b2e2e895380fa6acd537c2894e490e-Paper-Conference.pdf))
4. **Erasing the Invisible** (NeurIPS 2024 competition, UMD). 2,722 submissions / 298 teams, black-box and beige-box tracks; top-5 teams removed watermarks from >= 89% of images with high visual quality; the winner reached 95.7% using adaptive VAE evasion (beige-box) and clustered img2img diffusion with controlled noise + caption priors (black-box); a 5th-place entry simply **overwrote** StegaStamp with a second watermark. ([tech report, OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BeFjjyzWOJ), [first-place arXiv 2508.21072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21072), [overwriting attack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.01474))
5. **UnMarker** - Kassis & Hengartner (U. Waterloo), IEEE S&P 2025. First practical *universal* no-box attack: two adversarial optimisations on spectral amplitudes (a high-frequency stage for classical/learned marks, a low-frequency filter-based stage for semantic marks), no detector feedback, no knowledge of the scheme. Evaluated on StegaStamp, Stable Signature, Tree-Ring, Yu1/Yu2, HiDDeN, PTW, Gaussian Shading, PRC, VINE **and SynthID (via Imagen on Vertex AI)**: SynthID detection fell from ~100% to ~21% (79% success); best surviving semantic scheme 43%. Google acknowledged and paid a VRP bounty. Cost: >= 32 GB GPU. ([arXiv 2405.08363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08363), [IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/11023178/11023253/11023303.pdf), [code](https://github.com/andrekassis/ai-watermark), [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-watermark-remover))
6. **Black-Box Forgery Attacks on Semantic Watermarks** - Muller et al., CVPR 2025 (oral). *Imprinting* (pull a cover image's latent toward a watermarked image's latent in an unrelated proxy model) and *Reprompting* (invert a watermarked image, regenerate with a new prompt) forge Tree-Ring/RingID/Gaussian-Shading-style marks with a single reference image and a different model. Not applicable to SynthID directly (post-hoc), but it killed the "semantic watermarks are unforgeable" assumption. Follow-ups: theoretical treatment and a scheme-agnostic forged-sample detector at ICML 2026 ("Rethinking Forgery Attacks... A Geometric Distortion Perspective"). ([arXiv 2412.03283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03283), [code](https://github.com/and-mill/semantic-forgery), [ICML 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29807))
7. **WMCopier** - Dong et al., NeurIPS 2025. Trains an unconditional diffusion model on watermarked outputs to model the watermark distribution, then imprints it into clean images by shallow inversion; deceives open-source and closed-source systems (Amazon's Titan watermark is named). ([arXiv 2503.22330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22330))
8. **Transferable one-shot forging via image preference models** - Soucek et al. (ETH SRI Lab), NeurIPS 2025. A ranking model trained on procedurally generated images can both remove and forge post-hoc watermarks from a single watermarked example. ([arXiv 2510.20468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20468), [SRI](https://www.sri.inf.ethz.ch/publications/soucek2025forging))
9. **Coding limits** - Francati, Goonatilake, Pawar, Venturi, Ateniese, "The Coding Limits of Robust Watermarking for Generative Models" (2025, rev. May 2026). For alphabet size q, no sound scheme can detect tampering once more than 1-1/q of symbols change: **binary watermarks cannot survive >50% bit modification**, regardless of cryptographic strength. Validated on the PRC watermark. ([arXiv 2509.10577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10577), [IACR ePrint](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1620))
10. **Removal attack on latent/PRC watermarks** - Lee, Fang, Wang, Chang, CCS 2025: boundary information leaked by watermarked-object locations reduces required distortion up to 15x vs white-noise; defence is a secret transform hiding the boundary. ([arXiv 2509.11745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11745))
11. **DeMark** (Jan 2026): query-free black-box attack using compressive-sensing sparsification in latent space; 8 schemes, detection 100% -> 32.9% average. ([arXiv 2601.16473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16473))
12. **RAVEN** - Shamshad, Lukas, Nandakumar (MBZUAI), CVPR 2026: watermark removal as novel-view synthesis with a frozen img2img diffusion model plus cross-view correspondence attention; **mean TPR@1%FPR 0.026 across 15 watermarks**. ([arXiv 2601.08832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08832))
13. **FMDiffWA** (Apr 2026): frequency-domain modulated diffusion attack that selectively modulates watermark-bearing bands inside the diffusion sampler. ([arXiv 2604.22220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22220))
14. **"Removing the Watermark Is Not Enough"** - Goonatilake & Ateniese (May 2026): six SOTA removers across four attack families all leave forensic traces; an independent ResNet-50 detector distinguishes removal-processed images at >98% TPR @ 1% FPR. Important nuance for anyone claiming "clean" removal. ([arXiv 2605.09203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09203))
15. **"Do Modern Post-Hoc Watermarking Methods Beat Broken-Arrows?"** - Gesny & Giboulot (Inria, May 2026): fair comparison of classic (Broken Arrows, BOWS-2 era spread-spectrum) vs neural post-hoc schemes under classic augmentations and modern attacks; **classic watermarking wins on security while keeping comparable robustness** when an extremely low FPR is not the priority. Directly relevant to Section 4. ([arXiv 2605.27135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27135), [Broken Arrows](https://jis-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1155/2008/597040))
16. **MarkNull** - Aug 2026 (claimed USENIX Security 2026 by secondary sources; not verified). No-box, on-manifold latent manipulation using a public proxy model; introduces a Noise-Latent Alignment Score to decorrelate the latent from the embedded watermark. Across post-hoc, fine-tuning and initial-noise schemes it drives mean bit accuracy to 53.14% (~random); the amortised MarkNull-A runs in 0.50 s/image; the authors state it **"successfully compromises Google's SynthID-Image"** and transfers to video. ([arXiv 2608.10166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10166))
17. **Reverse-SynthID** (community, 2026, GitHub aloshdenny/reverse-SynthID). Pure signal-processing "reverse engineering": 100 black + 100 white reference generations per resolution plus 6 solid-colour consensus sets to average out content and estimate a spectral codebook; claims a 90%-accurate spectral detector and a 7-stage removal pipeline (VAE regeneration, elastic/geometric warps, colour shifts, FFT subtraction, JPEG) that defeats the Gemini detector on `gemini-3.1-flash-image-preview` / `nano-banana-pro-preview` images with "visually lossless" output. **Validation is 20 images**; treat as anecdotal, but it is the closest thing to a public averaging-attack on SynthID. ([repo](https://github.com/aloshdenny/reverse-SynthID))
18. **remove-ai-watermarks** (community, 2026, wiltodelta). Documents oracle-verified img2img removal thresholds (OpenAI's SynthID cleared at strength ~0.05, Google's at ~0.15 in their tests), notes seed non-determinism near threshold, that photoreal vs flat graphics behave oppositely, and that forensic classifiers still flag the outputs (echoing Goonatilake & Ateniese). ([docs/synthid.md](https://github.com/wiltodelta/remove-ai-watermarks/blob/main/docs/synthid.md))
19. **Spoofing** in the strict sense (making a non-Google image test positive) has been demonstrated generically for post-hoc marks (items 7-8) and for text SynthID (DITTO, distillation-based spoofing, [arXiv 2510.10987](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10987)); I did not find a published, oracle-verified spoof of SynthID-Image specifically as of Sept 2026. Also note SynthID-Text-specific robustness work ([arXiv 2508.20228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20228), [ETH SRI "Probing SynthID-Text"](https://www.sri.inf.ethz.ch/blog/probingsynthid)).

**Bottom line on SynthID security (Sept 2026):** robust to the benign transformation suite it was trained for (crop, resize, JPEG, colour, filters, screenshots to a degree), and among the strongest post-hoc schemes on public benchmarks; **not robust to determined no-box adversaries** using spectral optimisation (UnMarker), latent decorrelation (MarkNull), or view-synthesis regeneration (RAVEN). Google's own paper concedes this by scoping the threat model to limited-information, limited-compute adversaries and by relying on decoder retraining + key/versioning + C2PA layering rather than on unconditional robustness.

## 4. Practical design: a classical, no-training, open-source-friendly watermark robust to RST + colour transforms

The best-known design is essentially the Digimarc/Pereira-Pun architecture with a Solachidis-Pitas ring payload and Broken-Arrows-style detector discipline. Concretely:

### (a) Embed in the DFT magnitude of luminance
- Convert to a luma channel (Y of YCbCr or the L of Lab). Colour, brightness, contrast, hue and saturation edits then mostly become an affine change of Y that a normalised detector ignores; chroma-only filters do not touch the mark.
- Work on the **centered 2-D DFT magnitude** |F(u,v)| of a fixed-size analysis window (e.g., 512x512 after resizing the short side; or per tile, see (f)). Cyclic translation only changes phase, so the magnitude is translation-invariant; non-cyclic translation/crop changes the spectrum slightly but the mid-band statistics survive. ([MDPI print-cam DFT design](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/11/2/266), [Zheng 2003](https://home.cis.rit.edu/~cnspci/references/zheng2003.pdf))
- Keep **Hermitian symmetry**: modify coefficient (u,v) and (-u,-v) identically so the inverse FFT is real. Embed multiplicatively (|F| *= 1 + alpha * w) or additively on log-magnitude to keep the perturbation proportional to local spectral energy.

### (b) Log-polar / Fourier-Mellin for rotation and scale
- Rotation of the image rotates the magnitude spectrum by the same angle; isotropic scaling by s scales the spectrum by 1/s. In log-polar coordinates (log r, theta) these become **pure shifts**, so a second FFT (the Fourier-Mellin transform) or a **phase-correlation** between the log-polar map of the received spectrum and that of the reference template yields (delta log r, delta theta) directly. ([Zheng et al. 2003](https://home.cis.rit.edu/~cnspci/references/zheng2003.pdf), [Digimarc US7657057](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7657057))
- Practical advice, learned the hard way by the RST-literature: **do not embed in the log-polar domain and invert** (interpolation instability, aliasing, iterative inversion). Instead embed in the Cartesian DFT magnitude and use log-polar **only in the detector** to estimate (rotation, scale), then undo the geometry on the image (or on the spectrum) and correlate in the Cartesian domain. ([ACM CSUR survey](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1242471.1242473))
- OpenCV gives you `cv2.warpPolar(..., flags=cv2.WARP_POLAR_LOG)`, `cv2.phaseCorrelate`, `cv2.dft`; SciPy/NumPy `fft2`; `imreg_dft` (BSD) implements the whole log-polar registration pipeline if you want a reference. ([imreg_dft PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/imreg_dft/), [LogPolarFFTTemplateMatcher](https://github.com/Smorodov/LogPolarFFTTemplateMatcher))

### (c) Circularly symmetric ring templates in mid-frequency bands
- A ring (annulus) of radius r0 with constant or radially-keyed profile in |F| is **rotation-invariant by construction** (a rotated ring is the same ring) and **flip-invariant**; scaling changes only r0, which is recovered by a 1-D search over radius (or read off the log-polar shift). This is exactly why Tree-Ring's rings survive rotation while its "rand" and "zeros" patterns do not. ([Solachidis & Pitas](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Circularly-symmetric-watermark-embedding-in-2-D-DFT-Solachidis-Pitas/7830b2573df2f6f9ded44f61d4af7d9a1508719c), [Tree-Ring](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20030))
- Band choice: normalised radius ~0.15-0.40 of Nyquist. Below that you fight image energy (visible, and low-pass survives but so does the image's own structure that biases correlation); above that JPEG quantisation and blur erase you. Measure your target JPEG quality's effective cutoff and stay under it.
- Use **several concentric rings** with different keyed angular profiles: the ring set carries the synchronization *and* payload (Section h), and multiple radii give scale redundancy.

### (d) Keyed pseudo-random spread-spectrum sequences and correlation detection
- Secret key -> CSPRNG (e.g., `numpy.random.default_rng(seed_from_hmac(key))` or ChaCha) -> +-1 or Gaussian sequence w laid out along the ring(s) (angular index) and/or over the mid-band annulus. Without the key the mark is statistically white in the band; with the key the detector computes a linear correlation.
- **Whiten before correlating** (divide received log-magnitude by a local mean/median over the band, or apply a Wiener/Laplacian pre-filter). The host spectrum is highly coloured (1/f), and un-whitened correlation has huge image-dependent variance; whitening is the single biggest detector gain in the classical literature (Cox-Miller-Bloom). ([Cox et al. 1997](https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~ywang/MSS/HW2/CoxSpectrumWatermarking.pdf), [Digital Watermarking book](https://books.google.com/books/about/Digital_Watermarking.html?id=uJcEWRv-RRUC))
- Use **normalized correlation** (cosine similarity) so that gain changes (contrast, JPEG energy loss) do not move the statistic; Miller & Bloom give the exact FPR for a normalized-correlation detector under a spherical-Gaussian null as a ratio of two definite integrals depending only on threshold and dimension. ([Miller & Bloom 1999](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/10719724_11))
- Because each (u,v) and (-u,-v) are mirrored, use only a half-plane of the ring for the sequence and mirror it.

### (e) Template resynchronization: search at detect time
- Cheap route: **exhaustive search** over rotation in 0.5-1 deg steps (0-360, or 0-180 if your ring is symmetric) and scale in ~2-3% log steps (e.g., 0.5x-2x), computing normalized correlation at each hypothesis; take the max. Log-polar phase correlation gives you the (rotation, scale) peak in one FFT instead of a grid, then refine locally. Then run a translation search (phase correlation on the spatial pattern) if you also use spatial tiling.
- **Every hypothesis you test multiplies the false-positive probability.** Lichtenauer, Setyawan, Kalker & Lagendijk show the effective FPR of an exhaustive geometrical search grows roughly with the number of hypotheses and is worse than the naive Bernoulli model because hypotheses are correlated through the same image. Budget the number of hypotheses against your FPR target and raise the threshold accordingly (Section i). ([Lichtenauer et al., SPIE 2003](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/5020/1/Exhaustive-geometrical-search-and-the-false-positive-watermark-detection-probability/10.1117/12.503186.short))
- Alternative to exhaustive search: an explicit **keyed peak template** (Pereira & Pun): 8-16 impulses at keyed mid-band positions with pseudo-random phase. The detector finds local maxima, matches them to the known constellation under an affine model (RANSAC), and inverts the affine map, which also handles anisotropic scale and shear that log-polar cannot. Keep peaks weak and keyed; strong, unkeyed peaks are trivially notched out (template-removal attacks). ([DFT watermark patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20050036613A1/en), [Digimarc US6975744](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6975744))

### (f) Tiling / redundancy for crop robustness
- Embed the same spatial pattern in every NxN tile (Digimarc uses 128x128-ish tiles) so any crop containing a full tile - or, with cyclic tiling, any NxN window - carries the whole mark. Detection then averages the spectra (or folds the image modulo N before the FFT), which also boosts SNR by the number of tiles. Cyclic tiling turns arbitrary translation into cyclic translation, which the DFT magnitude ignores exactly. ([Digimarc US20030138127A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20030138127))
- For heavily cropped or edited images, complement with **keypoint-anchored** local marks (SIFT patches with polar mapping) - more complex but self-synchronising. ([ASIFT watermarking](https://doi.org/10.3390/app8030410))

### (g) Perceptual masking (JND) for imperceptibility
- Scale the spatial-domain perturbation per pixel by a **noise-visibility / JND map**: Voloshynovskiy's NVF (stochastic texture model: embed strongly in texture and edges, weakly in flat areas), Chou & Li's pixel-domain JND (luminance adaptation + texture masking), or Watson's DCT model (frequency sensitivity, luminance masking, contrast masking) if you work in 8x8 DCT blocks. Pixel Seal's "JND masking" is the modern learned echo of this. ([Voloshynovskiy et al. 1999](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/10719724_16), [Chou & Li 1995 JND](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Chou-and-Lis-pixel-domain-just-noticeable-difference-JND-model-1995_fig1_332691915), [Watson DCT model usage](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0705.1925))
- Implementation: compute the ideal DFT-domain modification, inverse-FFT to get the spatial perturbation p, multiply by the JND mask m, add m*p to the image, then **re-verify** in the detector loop (the masking slightly de-correlates the mark; iterate 2-3 times with a projection step to restore correlation - the "informed embedding" trick).
- Target PSNR 40-45 dB and SSIM >= 0.98 on photographs; flat/CG images need lower strength and are inherently harder (every library including invisible-watermark warns about homogeneous backgrounds).

### (h) Multi-bit vs zero-bit: encoding ~32-64 bits
- Zero-bit (presence only) is easiest and most robust: one keyed sequence, one normalized correlation. A provider ID can still be expressed as **one of K keys** (K sequences, pick argmax, threshold on the max) - this is what Tree-Ring/RingID do; FPR then scales with K.
- For a true 32-64-bit payload, use **CDMA-style code-division**: message bits b_1..b_L each modulate an independent keyed pseudo-random carrier c_l over the whole band (all carriers superposed), so every bit is spread over all coefficients (exactly the point SpreadMark re-discovers in a neural setting). Decode each bit by the sign of <r, c_l> after whitening. Because Gaussian carriers are nearly orthogonal in high dimension, cross-talk is tolerable; make them exactly orthogonal (Hadamard columns permuted by the key) if L is small relative to band size. ([Cox-Miller-Bloom "direct message coding vs CDMA"](https://books.google.com/books/about/Digital_Watermarking.html?id=uJcEWRv-RRUC), [SpreadMark 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03165))
- Always add **error correction plus a sync/check field**: e.g., 32 payload bits + BCH(63,36)/(127,64) or an LDPC code, plus a keyed 8-16-bit CRC used as a **second detection statistic** (a wrong-key/no-watermark image produces a random CRC). TrustMark's 100-bit / ~70-protected-bit BCH scheme is a good reference point for the overhead. ([TrustMark FAQ](https://github.com/adobe/trustmark/blob/main/FAQ.md))
- Detection statistic for the multi-bit case: correlate with the *decoded-and-re-encoded* codeword (or use the sum of |<r,c_l>| as a zero-bit "energy" statistic) so that presence detection does not depend on all bits being right.
- Prefer an **image-dependent key derivation** (e.g., key = HMAC(master, coarse perceptual hash)) if you want to blunt averaging/steganalysis attacks: the pattern is then content-adaptive across images while remaining recoverable from the received image's own hash (choose a hash robust to your transformation set). This follows the NeurIPS 2024 steganalysis paper's guideline (content-adaptive, multi-key). ([Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09026))

### (i) Distortion-invariant thresholds and false-positive calibration
- Normalized correlation makes the statistic invariant to gain; whitening makes its null distribution close to image-independent. Then either (1) use the Miller-Bloom closed form for the FPR of normalized correlation in dimension N (for N ~ 10^4 coefficients, a threshold of ~0.05-0.08 already gives FPR << 1e-6 per hypothesis), or (2) **calibrate empirically** on >= 10^4-10^5 unwatermarked images through your full detector including the geometric search, and set the threshold at the empirical quantile plus a safety margin - this is also what SynthID does in spirit with non-parametric conformal p-values. ([Miller & Bloom](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/10719724_11), [SynthID-Image snippets](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.09263))
- Apply a **Bonferroni-style correction** for the number of geometric hypotheses (angles x scales x flips x key candidates), and report the p-value, not just a binary. Because hypotheses are correlated, the true inflation is between 1 and the full count; measure it (Lichtenauer et al.).
- Make the detector **operating point explicit**: e.g., "TPR at 0.1% FPR after JPEG-50 + 5 deg rotation + 0.8x scale". Report distortions in a matrix like Section 3; do not average across distortions.
- Note Broken Arrows' "hypercone" detector: projecting onto a secret subspace and thresholding the angle (rather than the raw correlation) gives a statistic whose FPR is exactly computable and independent of the image - worth copying. ([Broken Arrows](https://jis-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1155/2008/597040))

### Putting it together (reference pipeline)
1. Key -> PRNG -> (i) ring profiles at radii r1..rk on the mid band; (ii) L orthogonal carriers over the annulus for payload bits; (iii) optional 8-16 weak peak template.
2. Luma, resize short side to 512 (keep a copy of the original size), FFT, add mark to magnitude (mirror-symmetric), inverse FFT, JND-mask, add, upsample residual back to original resolution (TrustMark's residual trick) and add to the original image.
3. Detector: luma, tile-fold or centre-window, FFT, whiten log-magnitude; log-polar phase-correlate against the ring template for (theta, s); small local refinement grid; undo geometry; normalized-correlate ring -> presence p-value; then decode payload bits, ECC-correct, verify CRC.
4. Try 4 rotations x 2 flips only if the ring-based estimate is ambiguous; count every extra hypothesis in the FPR budget.

Expected profile: robust to JPEG >= 40, resize 0.5-2x, rotation any angle, flips, colour/brightness/contrast/hue, moderate noise/blur, screenshots, crops down to ~1 tile; **not** robust to regeneration, adversarial spectral attacks or averaging over many images with the same key (mitigate with per-image key derivation and periodic key rotation). That matches what Inria's 2026 comparison found: a well-built classical scheme is as robust as neural ones for benign edits and harder to attack because there is no public decoder to invert.
